Where to Recycle a TV or Electronics Near Eagan (Dakota County 2026)

By Been There, Hauled That · November 18, 2026

Short answer: Take TVs and monitors to Dakota County's Recycling Zone at 3365 Dodd Rd in Eagan for $10 each; most other electronics are free there. Minnesota law bans tube (CRT) TVs and monitors from the trash. Flat-screen TVs 65 inches or smaller in working order can be donated to Bridging, and a junk removal pickup typically costs around $94 per TV.

Can I throw an old TV in the trash?

Not a tube TV or monitor. Minn. Stat. 115A.9565 says a person may not put an electronic product containing a cathode-ray tube in the trash, and that's been the law since 2006. Recycling every TV is the safe choice either way.

The Recycling Zone, step by step

The Recycling Zone (3365 Dodd Rd, Eagan; Wed 9–8, Thu 12–8, Fri 9–5, Sat 8–5; ID required, open to residents of the 7 metro counties). Bring your ID, pay $10 per TV or monitor, and leave computers, printers and small electronics for free. Wipe or remove hard drives first.

Big console or tube TV?

Those can weigh well over 100 pounds. If it's stuck in a basement, text us a photo. We carry it out, get it to a recycler, and 10–20% of the job goes to a local homeless shelter or dog rescue. Serving Eagan, Burnsville, Apple Valley, Lakeville, Farmington, Shakopee and Edina.

Questions people ask

How much does the Recycling Zone charge for a TV?

$10 per TV or monitor. Most other electronics are accepted free.

Is it illegal to throw away a TV in Minnesota?

TVs and monitors with cathode-ray tubes are banned from the trash under Minn. Stat. 115A.9565.

Where can I donate a TV near Eagan?

Bridging accepts working flat-panel TVs 65 inches or smaller. It does not take tube TVs.

Who can use the Recycling Zone?

Residents of Anoka, Carver, Dakota, Hennepin, Ramsey, Scott and Washington counties, with ID.
