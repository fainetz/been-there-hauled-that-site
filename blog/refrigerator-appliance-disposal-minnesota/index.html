Can You Throw Away a Refrigerator in Minnesota? Appliance Disposal in the South Metro

By Been There, Hauled That · November 18, 2026

Short answer: No. Minnesota Statute 115A.9561 bans major appliances like refrigerators, freezers, washers, dryers, stoves and water heaters from the trash and landfills; they must be recycled or reused. In the South Metro you can use an appliance recycler, a county drop-off ($15 at Hennepin County, $10 at Scott County), a bulky pickup through your hauler, or a junk removal company, which typically charges around $130 for a fridge.

What counts as a major appliance

Under Minnesota law: refrigerators, freezers, washers, dryers, stoves and ranges, dishwashers, water heaters, furnaces, air conditioners, dehumidifiers and similar household units. Fridges, freezers and AC units also hold refrigerant that has to be removed by a recycler before the metal is scrapped.

Can I donate a working appliance?

Sometimes. Goodwill-Easterseals Minnesota doesn't take large appliances, so working units usually go to a reuse store or a resale listing. If it still works, tell us when you send the photo and we'll try to place it before recycling it.

Why you can't just set it on the curb

Haulers can't legally put it in the trash, so a fridge at the curb either sits there or becomes an extra-fee pickup. Old fridges can also be a hazard for kids if the door is left on.

Getting it out of the house

The hard part is usually the stairs. We carry appliances out from basements, kitchens and garages anywhere in Eagan, Burnsville, Apple Valley, Lakeville, Farmington, Shakopee and Edina, get them to a recycler, and send 10–20% of the job to a local homeless shelter or dog rescue.

Questions people ask

Is it illegal to throw a refrigerator in the trash in Minnesota?

Yes. Minn. Stat. 115A.9561 bans major appliances from mixed municipal solid waste and landfills; they must be recycled or reused.

Does the Recycling Zone in Eagan take refrigerators?

No. The Recycling Zone takes small appliances but not large appliances, water heaters or microwaves.

How much does it cost to get rid of an old fridge?

County drop-offs charge about $10 to $15. Junk removal pickup averages around $130.

Do I need to remove the refrigerant myself?

No. Recyclers recover refrigerant. Don't cut lines yourself.
